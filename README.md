# CS 255: System Analysis and Design

## DriverPass Project

The DriverPass project involved designing a system for a client that wanted to improve how students prepare for their driving tests. DriverPass identified that many students were failing their driving tests because they were relying mostly on previous test questions instead of receiving enough actual preparation. The company wanted a system that would provide customers with online practice exams, training materials, and the ability to schedule on-the-road driving instruction. The system also needed to support employees and administrators by managing customers, appointments, drivers, vehicles, training packages, and other business information.

## What I Did Well

One part of the project that I think I did particularly well was translating the client's requirements into specific system requirements. Instead of only looking at what the customer would see, I considered how the entire system would need to work. For example, scheduling a driving lesson does not just involve selecting a date and time. The system also has to verify the availability of the customer, instructor, and vehicle before confirming the appointment. I think breaking requirements down this way helped create a design that better represented how DriverPass would actually operate.

## What I Would Revise

If I could revise one part of my work, I would spend more time refining the diagrams after completing the rest of the system design. Creating the diagrams showed me how easy it is to overlook smaller interactions when initially designing a system. After working through the complete project, I had a much better understanding of how the different parts of DriverPass connected. I would go back through each diagram and compare it against the final requirements to make sure every important interaction, decision, and relationship was represented as clearly as possible.

## Interpreting User Needs

I interpreted the user's needs by first identifying what DriverPass wanted the system to accomplish and then breaking those needs into individual requirements. I considered the different types of users, including customers, instructors, administrative employees, and IT staff, because each user interacts with the system differently. Those needs were then reflected in features such as account management, appointment scheduling, practice exams, instructor notes, reporting, and role-based access.

Considering user needs is important because a system can technically function correctly and still fail if it does not solve the user's actual problem. Understanding who will use the system and what they need to accomplish helps make sure the final design is useful instead of simply functional.

## My Approach to Software Design

My approach to designing software is to understand the problem before deciding how to build the solution. I would start by gathering requirements and identifying the users, their goals, and the major processes the system needs to support. From there, I would separate the requirements into functional and nonfunctional requirements and identify any assumptions or limitations that could affect the design.

I would also continue using multiple modeling techniques because this course showed me that different diagrams answer different questions. Process models are useful for understanding how information and activities move through a system, while object models help show the structure and relationships between different parts of the system. Activity and sequence diagrams can then provide more detail about specific workflows and interactions. Using these techniques together gives me a better understanding of a system before development begins and should reduce the amount of redesign needed later.
